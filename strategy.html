OUR STRATEGY

How we stay profitable when a trade round-trips.

Most traders buy, watch the price rise, then watch it fall right back to where they started — and walk away with nothing. We use delta-neutral hedges and covered options to bank gains along the way, so a round trip still ends in profit.

EXAMPLE 1 · BTC

Spot long + futures short: delta-neutral switching

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

We hold BTC on spot and hedge it with a futures short, then adjust the size of the hedge as the 4-hour RSI moves between oversold and overbought.

  1. a
    BTC $60,000 · neutral 4H RSI

    Starting point.

    $0
  2. b
    BTC $60,000

    Buy 1 BTC on spot and short 1 BTC on futures.

    The position is delta-neutral: price moves no longer change P&L, and we keep our good spot entry price.
    $0
  3. c
    BTC $57,000 · low 4H RSI

    Price drops. The spot loss is fully offset by the short.

    $0
  4. d
    BTC $57,000

    Close 50% of the short, banking $1,500 of profit on that half.

    We are now positioned to benefit from a bounce.
    $0
  5. e
    BTC $63,000 · higher 4H RSI

    Price rebounds. We mark this as an inflection point to bring the short back to 100%, but leave room for the move to run.

    +$3,000 (5%)
    or higher
  6. f
    BTC back to $63,000 and/or 4H RSI in our sell zone

    Re-add the short to return to delta-neutral.

    From here the $3,000 gain is locked in, whichever way price moves.
    +$3,000 (5%)
    or higher
  7. g
    BTC $60,000

    BTC round-trips back to the starting price.

EXAMPLE 2 · ETH

Spot long + covered calls: an income trade

ILLUSTRATIVE EXAMPLE

We hold ETH on spot and sell short-dated covered calls against it. The option premium is collected up front, and the calls offset roughly half of the position's price exposure. This example assumes a premium of about $60 per ETH (3%) for a 5–7 day at-the-money call.

  1. a
    ETH $2,000 · neutral 4H RSI

    Starting point.

    $0
  2. b
    ETH $2,000

    Buy 10 ETH on spot ($20,000) and sell 10 covered calls at a $2,000 strike, expiring in 5–7 days.

    About $600 of premium is collected up front.
    $0
  3. c
    ETH $2,200

    Sell 5 ETH on spot (+$1,000) and buy back half the calls (about −$750).

    The profit comes from option premium that has decayed over time.
    ≈ +$500 (2.5%)
  4. d
    ETH $2,000 at expiry, 5 days later

    The remaining 5 calls expire worthless and we keep all of their premium (about $300).

    ≈ +$550 (2.75%)
  5. e
    ETH $2,000

    ETH round-trips back to the starting price.

    ≈ +$550 (2.75%)

Price ends exactly where it started.
Buy-and-hold: $0.

Covered-call result: ≈ +$550 (2.75%) in one cycle
NOTES ON OPTION PREMIUM

How we use options

  1. We are selective. When selling covered calls and puts, premiums and liquidity can vary widely, so we only use this strategy when conditions are right.
  2. Iron condors. Hybrid "iron condor" trades also work as an effective delta-neutral trade.
  3. Doubling the premium. We occasionally buy spot and sell covered calls while at the same time selling spot and selling covered puts, effectively doubling the option premium we can collect.
  4. Always covered. We never sell uncovered options.
